VISION

Can Pepper see?

Yes. Two colour cameras and a depth sensor provided visual data for human interaction and perception. Modern object recognition and scene understanding can be added using a separate AI computer.

HEARING

Can Pepper listen?

Four microphones and speakers supported speech and sound-based interaction. A new voice pipeline could deliver more natural conversations, while keeping microphones opt-in.

MOVEMENT

Can Pepper navigate?

Pepper moved on a wheeled base, using lasers, sonar, infrared and bumpers to sense obstacles. Navigation depended on software and environment; reliable autonomous indoor routes require testing and mapping, not just a new language model.

Official 2015 launch specification: 795 Wh lithium-ion pack, approximately 12+ hours operation, speed up to 2 km/h. Actual runtime will depend on battery health and workload. SoftBank source.

Engineering reality check

Science first. Imagination always.

Battery

Two years in storage may have damaged the roughly 795 Wh pack. We need the correct 28.6 V / 8 A charger and a safe test. Never bypass lithium battery protection.

Software

We don't know whether this robot has NAOqi 2.5 or 2.9. Preserve the installed system before any reset. Archived firmware does not guarantee recovery without credentials.

AI and safety

Modern AI should run externally and request movements through a limited, supervised robotics interface. Keep the emergency stop and obstacle detection working.
