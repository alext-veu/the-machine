01 / The robot we found

👀 Vision

Two RGB cameras and a 3D depth sensor, depending on revision. Modern scene recognition and object descriptions can run on an external AI computer.

🎙 Hearing

Four microphones and built-in speakers support listening and speech. A new speech-to-text → AI → text-to-speech pipeline could enable much richer conversations.

🛞 Navigation

Omnidirectional wheeled movement, laser sensing, sonar, infrared and bumpers help detect obstacles. Navigation and reliable room mapping require dedicated, tested robotics software.

🔋 Endurance

Original literature cites roughly 12 hours active operation with a 795 Wh battery. After long storage, the actual runtime is unknown until inspected and tested.

Common Pepper 1.8 hardware is documented with Intel Atom E3845, 4 GB RAM and 32 GB eMMC; earlier versions differ. Pepper is around 1.2 m tall and was designed for human interaction, not lifting heavy objects. Original launch specifications · Hardware sheet.

02 / Finding the right charger

Original charger class reported as Delta ADP-235CR-A / ADP-235AR-A, around 28.6 V DC, 8 A (~229 W), with a Pepper-specific twist-lock plug. Compatibility, connector pinout and polarity must be checked with the seller before buying.

Battery safety comes first

Pepper's nominal battery specification is approximately 26.46 V, 30 Ah, 795 Wh lithium-ion. Two years without charge could mean deep discharge or BMS protection, but we have not tested it. Do not force-charge, directly power the pack, bypass protection or open cells. Stop if there is swelling, leakage, smell or heat. IRIS Robotics service is a European repair lead; an earlier €250 + VAT diagnostic indication is unverified and needs a new quote.

03 / Recovery: do not erase a working robot

  1. Photograph and inspect: exterior, model labels, charging port and tablet. Keep identifying labels and serial numbers out of public photographs.
  2. Source the compatible charger: verify Pepper-specific connector, output, polarity and UK mains suitability.
  3. Power on safely: note lights, spoken messages, movement and touchscreen. Do not attempt undocumented firmware procedures.
  4. Discover the network: use an isolated LAN; supported NAOqi 2.5 releases may announce their IP after a short chest-button press.
  5. Attempt authorised access: documented NAOqi defaults are nao / nao on some installations, but may have been changed. Do not brute-force.
  6. Back up before changing: record firmware, apps, configuration and access status. If locked, consult vendor procedures or specialist service rather than blindly resetting.
  7. First safe action: supervised text-to-speech, then small head/arm gesture with emergency stop accessible.

04 / Understanding Pepper's software

NAOqi 2.5

Linux-based robot services, Choregraphe development environment, Python 2.7 and C++ SDK. Often attractive for hobby and research integration through the original robot APIs.

NAOqi 2.9

Android / QiSDK and Java/Kotlin app development. Different application model: don't assume 2.5 Python apps will work after an upgrade.

NAOqi 2.5 reference documentation · Archived SDK and firmware resources · 2.9 reset · 2.9 upgrade precautions. An archived firmware image does not automatically unlock a robot with an unknown password.

05 / Giving Pepper a new AI brain

Recommended: leave Pepper's original safety and motor-control electronics in place. Connect a laptop, Raspberry Pi 5 or Intel mini PC over an isolated LAN. Run a speech recogniser, language model, text-to-speech, optional vision and a strict action-permission bridge externally.

Happy house robot

Friendly questions, jokes, calendar reminders and teaching exercises with Boris.

Curious explorer

Opt-in object recognition, a carefully mapped room, sensor readings and supervised indoor navigation experiments.

The Collective

A playful, Borg-inspired modular-upgrade concept: sensors, dashboards and replaceable AI modules. A tribute inspired by science fiction, not an official Star Trek product.

Safety design: AI text is never a raw motor command. Allow only approved behaviours and constrained movements. Keep emergency stop functional; do not expose legacy robot services or stream people's images without consent. Keep credentials outside public repositories.
